Most people hide their work until it's polished. Most people hide their failures completely.

I chose a different path. Every project here includes what I learned, what didn't work, and what I'd do differently. Some projects are archived before they ship. Some blog posts document spectacular failures.

## Why Transparency Matters

**You learn faster.** When you know people are watching, you write better code. You explain your thinking more clearly. You're forced to confront your assumptions.

**Others learn from your mistakes.** If I don't share that the personal assistant project failed, no one learns why privacy-first is hard. If I only show successes, others waste time on the same mistakes.

**You attract collaborators.** People want to work with builders who are honest about both successes and failures. They want to learn from real experience, not highlight reels.

**You build a network of support.** The people who follow your journey genuinely care about your success, not just your wins. They celebrate the progress and support you through the setbacks.

## The Fear

Learning in public is scary. You expose yourself to criticism. You admit what you don't know. You ship incomplete work.

But I've found that the people who matter—other builders, potential collaborators, thoughtful observers—respect the courage more than they judge the imperfection.

## The Practice

I commit to:
- Shipping early and often
- Documenting learnings, not just results
- Sharing failures with the same detail as successes
- Treating feedback as a gift, not an attack

If you're building something, I hope you'll join me in learning in public. The AI community is stronger when we're honest about what we're building and what we're learning.

Start small. Share one thing. You'll be surprised by the response.
