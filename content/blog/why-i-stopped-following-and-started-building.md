## The thing nobody says out loud

Open LinkedIn right now. Scroll for 30 seconds.

I'd bet you'll find at least one post promising that some AI prompt, tool, or "system" will save you hours, grow your business, or change your life — if you just follow, like, and repost.

I'm not being cynical. Some of those posts are genuine. But most of them are exploiting something real: we all want shortcuts. We all want to believe there's a faster way. And AI makes that fantasy feel more plausible than ever.

I fell for it too. For months I consumed content about AI — tutorials, breakdowns, "how I made X with GPT" posts, YouTube deep dives. I got better at talking about AI. I got worse at actually doing anything with it.

## The moment I decided to stop

I can't point to a single moment. But I remember sitting at my desk one evening, having spent an hour reading about AI tools, and realising I had nothing to show for any of it. No code. No product. No real understanding of how these systems actually work.

I knew what "prompt engineering" meant. I couldn't build anything with it.

That gap — between knowing about something and actually being able to do it — is exactly what AI content on the internet is terrible at closing. Because content is optimised for engagement, not learning. And engagement means making you feel informed without requiring real effort.

## What I decided to do instead

Build things. Actual things. For myself.

Not demos. Not tutorials I follow step-by-step. Real products that solve problems I actually have, that I have to debug when they break, that I have to maintain when they stop working.

The first one was a Polish learning app. I'd just finished Duolingo and could barely hold a conversation. Duolingo is great at drilling vocabulary in isolation — useless for learning a language in context. So I built a web app that uses Claude to generate contextual Polish lessons on demand.

It was messy. It took longer than expected. Half of what I tried didn't work the first time. And I learned more in two weeks of building than in months of reading about AI.

## What actually changes when you build

A few things happen when you stop consuming and start making:

**You discover what you don't know.** Reading about APIs is nothing like integrating one. You hit errors you didn't expect, edge cases nobody wrote about, and constraints that only show up in real usage. Each one teaches you something real.

**You get honest feedback from reality.** When something breaks, it breaks. There's no algorithm deciding whether your understanding is good enough — the code either works or it doesn't. That's clarifying in a way that content never is.

**The documentation actually makes sense.** I read the Claude API docs when I was building, and I understood them immediately — because I had a specific problem I was trying to solve. The same docs would have felt abstract if I'd read them without a project.

**You have something to show.** Not a repost. Not a screenshot of someone else's output. Something you made, that works, that you understand.

## This site is the continuation of that decision

I'm going to keep building. One project at a time. Practical tools for my own daily life — nothing fancy, nothing viral-bait. A personal assistant. A budget tracker. A running companion.

And I'm going to document everything here — the wins, the mistakes, the dead ends. Not because I'm an expert. Because I'm figuring it out, and I think there's value in watching someone figure something out in real time.

If you're tired of AI hype, you're in the right place.

No shortcuts. Just building.
