There's a land grab happening around "GEO" — generative engine optimization, the art of getting your content cited inside AI answers from ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Google's AI overviews. Most of the advice I read was just SEO advice with the letters rearranged. When I started building a tool to optimize for both, I learned quickly that they are not the same game, and treating them as one makes both worse.

## Different judges, different rules

Classic search optimizes for a *ranking*. The page is the unit. A lot of the game is about authority signals — links, keywords, freshness — that decide where you land in a list of ten blue links.

Answer engines don't show a list. They synthesize one answer and maybe cite a few sources. The unit isn't the page; it's the *quotable claim*. To get cited, your content has to be the thing the model reaches for when it needs a clean, attributable fact — clear, specific, well-structured, and easy to lift a sentence out of.

Those two targets pull in different directions more often than you'd think.

## Where they conflict

The classic SEO move of padding a page to "cover the topic comprehensively" — long intros, repeated keywords, a 2,000-word preamble before the actual answer — is exactly what makes content *hard* to cite. An answer engine doesn't want your comprehensive page. It wants the one precise sentence that answers the question, and it'll skip the page that buries it.

Conversely, the tight, factual, well-attributed structure that answer engines love can read as too thin for traditional ranking, which still rewards depth and dwell time.

Optimize blindly for one and you can quietly hurt the other.

## What the tool actually does

That conflict is why SEO / GEO Engine scores content against **two separate rubrics** instead of one blended score. One rubric is the familiar search checklist. The other asks GEO-specific questions: Is there a clean, liftable claim? Is it specific and attributable? Could a model quote this without distorting it?

Every recommendation is then labeled by which channel it helps — and flagged when a change helps one at the other's expense. The point isn't to chase a single number. It's to make the trade-off visible so you can choose.

## The takeaway

GEO isn't SEO with a new acronym. Search ranks pages; answer engines quote claims. The tactics overlap in places and contradict in others, and the only way to optimize for both honestly is to stop pretending they're the same metric.

SEO / GEO Engine is still in progress, but the two-rubric split is the part I'm most sure about — because it's the part that stops good SEO advice from being bad GEO advice.
